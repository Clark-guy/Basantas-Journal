4/18/26 - Hello World!

Hello hello!

I'm going to assume at this point that if you're reading this, you likely know who I am, at least kind of. If not, take a look at the "About" tab on the left.

As this is my first blog entry on the new site, I'll be giving a quick run-down of my time here in Nepal since I left.

I left home about three months ago to join the Peace Corps in Nepal. since then, I've effectively been in 4 different stages of my Peace Corps journey: Pre-departure, IO, PST, and service (in progress). I'll give a quick explanation of each.


Pre-departure:

Flying from Boston to Philly, staying overnight there for some general Peace Corps presentations / discussions. This is where I met the 50 or so other individuals that comprise Peace Corps Nepal Cohort N211, or the 211th group of Peace Corps volunteers to arrive here. This stage was really only a couple of days.


IO:

Following pre-departure, we took a bus from Philly to Newark, took a plane from Newark to Turkey, bummed around in the airport for like 10 hours, and then took a plane from Turkey to Kathmandu. Upon arriving in Kathmandu, IO began - I'm pretty sure it stands for Initial Orientation, but I could be wrong about that . IO was roughly the first week or so we stayed in Kathmandu, during which we spent a lot of time listenting to various people talk about what to expect here. It was in IO that I had my first experience with bucket showers, eating with my hand, and using squat toilets. These are all fun when you get the hang of it!

Click to see a monkey


PST:

This is where I've been for most of the time since leaving the states. After a week of IO near Kathmandu, we all took busses to Panauti, a medium sized town situated a few hours South East of Kathmandu. This is where I began learning Nepali, met and stayed with my first host family, and taught in a Nepali school for the first time (the first three times actually!) There is a lot that I could recount from PST but I am trying to keep this update relatively brief, and may be failing that already. I'll leave you with a few photographs of my time there.

Swear In and Service:

At the end of PST, we waved goodbye to our host families and were bussed off to Kathmandu to swear in officially as Peace Corps Volunteers, trainees no more. You can see a few photos of us on the US Embassy in Nepal's Instagram Post. After a week in Kathmandu, me and Leo, the other volunteer serving in Gorkha District, were driven out to Gorkha Bajaar, our district center. We stayed a couple of nights there, and then were picked up by jeeps to go to our permanent sites, where I have been for the past 2 and a half weeks. This brings us to today - because this is a little bit of a catch-up post, I'm not going to go into huge detail about my site and host family now - I will instead leave a few pictures to give a general idea, with more to come later. Thank you for following along, and please bear with me as I make more changes to the site.
