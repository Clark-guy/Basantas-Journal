Ate and Left No Crumbs

I’m concerned with my ability to finish projects I’ve started.

I know that I need to focus on my teaching, while also have begun toying with 3d graphics in 3js AND blender, have been trying to comprehend the Mandelbrot set while considering other adjacent instances of mathematical beauty, and want to read and comprehend both satoshi Nakamoto’s whitepaper on cryptocurrency and the pivotal white paper “attention is all you need” for attention mechanisms with relation to artificial intelligence and deep learning. Yesterday I got it in my head that it would be a good idea to play soccer every day after school. As I began writing this out, I realized it could make a good blog post, on the blog that I still have much work to do on before I consider it complete.

While I’m cognizant that my quick endearment with big tasks (and often tasks outside my range of comprehension) can be beneficial, it is a double edged sword. One of the biggest issues I've faced with my own productivity in life is failure to complete what I've started. This issue takes many forms in my life - consistently being a B or C student, jobs that I didn't put my all into, a github full of half baked projects resting on libraries that have since aged out of the versions they were built on, now in a state that would take more backtracking than anything just to get working again, if I even had the interest. My parent's garage is now full of most of my earthly belongings at this point, and a significant percentage of those earthly belongings are hardware projects or amplifiers that I began assembling, hit a wall, and then gave up on.

Frankly, that's why something like this site is so satisfying to me. Static pages, not a lot to break, quick to make and easy to fix if and when I do break something. I made it look kinda pretty with some CSS, and that's it, call it a day. At the same time, this project scales not technically but artistically - I don't need to implement more to make this site worth it, I just need to journal. That takes the pressure off of building some grand scale thing that I don't know if I'd even be capable of building.

There's definitely something to be said about giving yourself small projects to feel productive day to day. This applies broadly not just to technical projects, but also to anything in your life you can complete - chores are a good example. It's incredibly satisfying to get a chore out of the way that you've been putting off. I've been finding a lot of satisfaction in hand washing my laundry here - not only learning the skill of how to do it so that my shirts don't smell like hot dogs after drying, but also just the simple action of getting it out of the way, folding everything up, and making it look presentable for myself to wake up to each morning.

Still, larger projects have their place as well. It is incredibly satisfying to work on something over a long period of time, or even more so to work on something as part of a team. I feel as though I want to decide on a project that I work on over a longer period of time, but I know that eventually my interest will wane. I need to learn how to keep myself focused on a single goal over a long period of time. I'm not sure if it's an issue of discipline or attention, or just finding something that is so engaging to me that I don't feel interested in pivoting. It may be worth relating a project to the same way that I've viewed relationships in the past - with peaks and valleys, but overall a positive thing that is worth the effort of maintaining. I think when I hit a valley with a project I can quickly be dissuaded and move on. I need to find a way to push through those low points for the sake of whatever project I'm working on.

I think much of the time it won't even be a low point necessarily but just a decision to be made that I don't want to make. I am prone to being affected by decision paralysis - it's worth remembering to me that making no decision at all is a decision in it's own right. Just a moment ago I was trying to decide whether I wanted to actually post this blog post, and nearly shuffled it off into my notes app never to be seen again. Frankly, I don't think I will link this blog post because of it's slightly off topic nature, but anyone tech savvy will still be able to find it in the site's directory. That said I think I'll mostly be keeping this one for my own review.

As always, thanks for reading, even if this is kind of a weird one.
